Damage on the bar

Miss feedback for the scored run. Hypothesis: a wrong answer should be felt before it’s read — in scored mode a miss genuinely subtracts points, so the bar can react like a health bar taking damage instead of politely re-easing its width. Six directions below, each borrowing a different retro damage idiom. Same three-colour system as the shipped quiz — judge every direction against every set, because the flash contrast changes with the surface.

Guardrails

  • Flash budget. Every direction stays at two or fewer flashes per event — inside WCAG 2.3.1’s three-flashes-per-second limit with margin, and the flashing area is a 10px strip, far below the harmful-area threshold.
  • Reduced motion. All six collapse to an instant width change with no flash — the toggle above previews it, and prefers-reduced-motion is honoured for real.
  • The bar stays decorative. As shipped, the bar is aria-hidden; the numeric points label is the accessible reading. The animation may never become the only signal of a miss — the verdict from onCheckAnswer already recolours the option and the label.
  • Width, not scaleX. The shipped bar animates width deliberately: a transform would stretch the sprite caps and defeat the 4px fill quantisation. The element is tiny, so the layout cost is noise — keep that trade in any port.
  • Continuous line, never cells. The scored bar’s standing rule. Every direction keeps the fill a single run — damage may recolour or clip it, never segment it.
  • Follow-ups worth a second lab: floating damage numbers (−12 drifting off the bar tip), a paired sound tick, and whether CORRECT deserves a mirrored micro-celebration or should stay calm so damage keeps the spotlight.
