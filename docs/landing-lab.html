1 · Decisions to take
LP1 Rec: do it

The catalogue moves onto the landing page

The insight itself, recorded: on mobile the drills sit three taps behind a menu, and the menu is the only signpost. The homepage’s job is “get people drilling”, the catalogue is thirteen links — small enough to BE the page. Content-as-navigation is how every course product solves this (Khan Academy’s course pages, Duolingo’s path): the index is the interface.

Hypothesis, measurable: surfacing the drills should raise the share of landing sessions that start a drill, and cut time-to-first-drill. The port tags every catalogue click (landing_drill_click) so both are readable in PostHog — see LP9.

LP2 Decided 2026-08-17: topic cards

Catalogue form — cards, rows, or ledger

Cards keep the topic as the unit (Gensler’s chapters; professors assign by set) — two columns on desktop, a clean stack on the phone, every drill visible inside its topic. Rows give each topic the full width — more air, more scroll, reads editorial. Ledger is the fastest scan of all thirteen drills (the syllabus reading) in the FAQ’s split-rail shape — but the topic identity thins to a group header.

Malik’s call: topic cards at both widths. The judgement moved in two passes the same day — first rows on desktop with cards on the phone, then, after more testing, cards everywhere: the two-column card grid scans better than six full-width bands, and one form at both widths is one component with no layout fork. Rows stay on the dial (and still reflow to cards on the phone) for comparison.

LP3 Decided 2026-08-17: chips

How much set colour

Plates paint each card in its set’s own surface — the landing page becomes the quilt, and the colour a student meets here is the colour their quiz screen wears (the palette as wayfinding, not decoration). Chips keep cards neutral and spend the set colour in 30 px gem icon-chips and tags — calmer, still keyed. Quiet is the control: no per-set colour, proves whether the colour earns its keep. Caution against plates: six pale surfaces at once is loud, and the footer’s colour moment follows two sections later. Malik’s call: chips — the caution won; the set colour stays in the gem icon-chips and tags, and the ground carries the scheme (see LP11). If plates ever return, the per-topic focus finding in § 2 comes with them.

LP4 Decided 2026-08-17: masthead

The hero compresses

Today’s hero (mascot + H1 + description) spends the whole first phone viewport saying “Master Formal Logic” with nothing to tap — the audit strip prints where the first drill lands under each hero. The compact masthead keeps the promise (display H1 in the type ink, one-liner under) and hands the fold to the catalogue. “None” is the brave reading — bar, eyebrow, drills — kept on the dial to test whether the H1 earns its height. The H1’s words stay; only its register changes.

LP5 Leaning off (the 08-17 pick leaves it off)

The mascot’s fate

The Rubik’s-cube kid is the page’s one piece of warmth, and it currently costs a third of the desktop hero and most of the phone fold. The dial keeps it small beside the masthead (and in the ledger’s rail on desktop), or drops it. A middle path for the port: keep it on desktop where the width is free, drop it on the phone where the fold is the scarce resource — the lab already does this in the ledger rail.

LP6 Malik kept verbatim — edit stays on the dial

The one-liner

Verbatim: “Generate endless, error-free exercises with smart step-by-step hints. Works offline, directly in your browser.” Two sentences, front-loads “generate” (the system’s feature) over the student’s job. Proposed: “Endless logic drills with step-by-step hints — free in your browser, online or off.” One breath, leads with the drills (which now sit directly below it), keeps every claim. “Free” does more work than “error-free” for a student deciding whether to scroll.

LP7 Judge — start ships, resume later

Entry guidance — START HERE, and continue?

A first-time student faces thirteen drills with no reading order. The START HERE gem on Set A’s first drill answers it in the catalogue’s own grammar (one gem, the topic’s own ink — it appears exactly once, so it stays a signal). The resume banner is the returning-student half: “continue where you left off” from localStorage — real value for a semester-long course, but it needs the quiz to write a last-drill key first, so it’s a follow-up port, simulated here to judge the shape.

Malik’s pick sets resume as the target state. Port order: the banner renders only when a last-drill key exists, so first-time visitors see a clean catalogue — ship the catalogue first, add the key write to the quiz, then the banner. Whether START HERE serves as the empty-state fallback for new visitors stays open.

LP8 Rec: menu stays, stakes drop

What happens to the exercises menu

The menu remains the global chrome — it is how you switch drills from a quiz page, the blog, anywhere that isn’t the homepage. Nothing here deletes it. But the landing page stops DEPENDING on it, which reframes the open D13 judgement (3d-f vs 3d-g): the sheet becomes secondary navigation, so that call loses urgency — take it calmly, delete /nav-preview when done. On the homepage, the bar’s “Exercises” item can simply scroll to the catalogue.

LP9 Rec: instrument before judging

Measurement — the hypothesis in PostHog

Events (snake_case per convention): landing_drill_click with topic_id, quiz_path, position, and source: 'landing_catalog'. Success looks like: share of homepage sessions reaching quiz_started up, median time-to-first-drill down, and mobile converging toward desktop. The existing sheet path keeps its own source, so the two routes are comparable in one insight.

LP10 Rec: carry D6 + the gems

Set letters, NEW, and the drill titles

Professors assign by set letter (the nav lab’s D6), so the letter stays visible: on the card header for single-set topics, on each drill for Informal (Q · R). The NEW gem carries over from the shipped grammar (Informal Fallacies wears it today). Drill titles use the panel rule from content/topics.ts — topic adjective off (“Translations: Easy”), full names for the single-title sets — with the WCAG 2.4.4 note in the port section.

LP11 Decided for the landing · D11 still open

Ground — Set L mint on the landing

Malik’s 08-17 pick grounds the landing in Set L’s mint — the first content surface to leave the white/cream lean, and consistent with the standing lean toward Set L as the brand primary. Consequences to hold: the page now reads mint (landing) → white (FAQ, shipped) → mint (footer) — judge that seam deliberately; the white cards + plum type all derive cleanly (audit strip), and the focus ring on mint is a DERIVED colour (green fails 3:1 there — the 08-15 finding). This front-runs D11 for one surface; the global scheme decision (and the token swap in marketingTheme.ts / topics.ts / footer.tsx) stays its own call.

2 · Accessibility + port notes
  • Colour tiers are the shipped constants: each topic’s surface/ink/meta/body/hair come verbatim from content/topics.ts (already ensure-contrast derived and checked in). What the lab adds: tags and body text on NEUTRAL grounds (chips/quiet modes) are re-derived at render against the actual card ground, and a per-topic focus colour — the system green fails 1.4.11’s 3:1 floor on several set surfaces (the audit strip prints the worst case), extending the 08-15 focus finding. If plates ship, the derived focus tier belongs in content/topics.ts next to the others.
  • Semantics: the catalogue is content, not nav — a section with a heading, topics as an ul, drills as plain links. Link names must survive out of context (WCAG 2.4.4): “Translations: Easy” alone doesn’t, so the port gives each link an aria-label of the full catalogue title (“Syllogistic Translations: Easy”) while the visible text stays short. The START/NEW gems get sr-only expansions.
  • Focus: drill rows stay UNCLIPPED (the gem clip lives on a ::before fill layer) so :focus-visible draws a clean rectangle — canon: a clipped element paints no outline. Card shadows ride a wrapper as filter: drop-shadow (the Viewport rule). Targets: rows ≥46 px, pills ≥44 px.
  • Port shape: a server component (components/landing/catalog.tsx) reading topics + drillTitle from content/topics.ts — the IA and colours already live there; no new data file. The hero edit touches components/header.tsx only. Hover fills reuse the globals.css gem construction from the nav port. The mascot’s conditional render is a hidden sm:block, not a second image.
  • Events: landing_drill_click — topic_id, quiz_path, position (1-based across the page), source: 'landing_catalog'. The resume banner (when it lands) writes landing_resume_click and needs the quiz to persist a last_drill key first.
  • H1 semantics: hero · today and masthead keep the H1 (“Master Formal Logic”). Hero · none must still ship an H1 — sr-only above the catalogue — or the page’s outline starts at the FAQ’s h2.
  • The fold marker is an estimate (iPhone 14 Safari, large URL bar ≈660 px of 844) — treat it as a budget, not a measurement; the Vercel preview on a real phone is the truth. Capture note: phones render through the 390 px frame — headless Chrome clamps windows below ≈500 px wide.
  • Deep-linkable state: landing-lab.html?form=ledger&color=chips&hero=none — any dial key overrides the default, for sharing exact compositions and headless capture.
