Footer band lab

Malik, 2026-08-23, in three notes: rethink the footer illustration — a larger pattern, or the quilt pixel we’re using for social media; then the quilt should feature the sets’ colours, like the social media artifact; then this is what was decided for social media, and it should inform the footer and the icons.

Taken together those three collapse into one answer, and it needed no new design decision at all. The decided cover is Set L · Sets · full: ground #CFF6DD, dominant #3F0167, a nine-colour pool, accent rate 75%. The footer already runs that exact ground and that exact dominant — GROUND and TYPE in components/footer.tsx are the same two hexes. So the band can wear the cover’s recipe verbatim; the only open number is scale, because the band is 56px and the cover is not.

Two structural facts behind the rest of this sheet. Camo cannot do it — camoBody reads exactly one entry from its pool (const c1 = pool[0] || ink), so a camo band is a two-colour object however you tune it; only quiltPixBody samples the whole pool. And the nine-colour pool is ungated on purpose — see the notes; that call is the brand lab’s, already made and already reasoned.

The gate, and why the cover drops it

I got this wrong on the first pass tonight and the correction is the most useful thing on the page. Measuring the seven set surfaces against the footer’s mint with quiltAccentPool’s 0.17 floor, only two survive — from which I concluded the surfaces could not carry the band and proposed the tab colours instead. The brand lab had already been here and reached the opposite conclusion, for a better reason. From docs/brand-lab.html, dated 2026-08-12/13:

THE GATE IS A STUDY-SURFACE RULE, and the cover is not a study surface… quiltAccentPool rejects anything within 0.17 of the ground. That exists so a pattern cannot muddy a surface a student is reading against for twenty minutes… The 2024 cover is the counter-evidence: its cream tiles sit on a near-white ground at almost no separation, and it reads fine, because a quilt tile is a SHAPE with a hard edge and a gap around it. Edge does the work that contrast does on a text surface.

The ungated modes keep the ink gate — a tile the same colour as the dominant really does vanish, since there is no edge between them — and drop the ground gate. That is what produces the decided nine: six set surfaces (all but Set L’s own mint, which is the ground) plus three set inks. The other three inks are rejected at 0.021, 0.082 and “is the dominant”. Reproduced here exactly — the swatch strip on each direction is computed, not typed.

A footer band is the same kind of surface as a cover, not the same kind as a quiz screen. It is 56 decorative pixels at the very bottom of the document, below the last link, carrying no text and sitting under nothing anyone reads. The rule that protects a twenty-minute study surface has no jurisdiction there, by the brand lab’s own argument. So the band gets the ungated nine.

What my tab-colour proposal was actually worth: the measurement in it is still true — the tab colours are the only tier where all seven sets are simultaneously gate-clearing and mutually distinguishable (closest pair 0.101, against the surfaces’ 0.041 and eight collapsing pairs). That matters if the goal is every set identifiable. It is kept below as an alternative. But it was solving a problem the brand work had already dissolved, and “like the social media artifact” means the artifact’s own recipe, not a better one.

The one number that is genuinely open: scale

A quilt cell is 62 × scale px and rows are laid from y=0, so a band whose height is not a whole multiple of the pitch ends in a cropped row. The strip therefore has one exact answer, scale = band height / 62, which at the shipped 56px is 0.90 and fills it with a single uncropped row. The cover’s own 1.53 puts a 95px cell in a 56px strip — one sliver, shown below. So the band and the covers share the pattern and the palette but not the number: the same object at two sizes, which is what a mark does.

The band-height dial is live. If the band grows, the natural scale grows with it: 0.90 at 56px, 1.16 at 72px, 1.42 at 88px. At 95px the band would wear the cover’s literal 1.53 — worth knowing, though a 95px band is nearly double the current footprint and Malik’s brief said the footprint stays.

Rate 75%, not 28%

The decided cover runs rate: 0.75 where every quiz screen runs 0.28. The number is not the share you get — cells whose left or upper neighbour is already an accent are rejected, so the rule’s own ceiling is about 52% — but the difference between 0.28 and 0.75 is the difference between a plum band with colour in it and a band that is the sets. Since the point of the change is to feature the sets’ colours, the cover’s rate is the right one to carry across, and the 0.28 version is shown so the gap is visible rather than asserted.

Recommendation

The cover recipe, verbatim, at scale 0.90. Quilt · pixel, ground #CFF6DD, dominant #3F0167, the decided nine-colour pool, rate 0.75. It satisfies all three of Malik’s notes at once, introduces no colour the brand has not already approved, changes no ground, and keeps the band’s 56px footprint. The only thing it changes is register — the shipped band is a texture you read as tone, this is a row of discrete marks you read as a border — and that change is the point rather than a side effect, because the border is what the covers look like.

The one edit is inside bandSvg() in components/footer.tsx: swap camoBody('camo', …) for patternBody('quilt', …), pass the nine as the pool with the ungated filter, set scale: 0.90 and rate: 0.75.
