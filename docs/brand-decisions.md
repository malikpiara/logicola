# Social identity — decisions and open items

**Status: PROPOSED, 2026-08-12.** Nothing is uploaded yet. The working lab
is `docs/brand-lab.html`; the exportable set is `docs/brand/`, regenerated
by `pnpm brand:assets`.

Companion documents: `docs/pixel-ui.md` (shape and iconography contract),
`docs/color-system.md` / `docs/color-handoff.md` (the palette's reasoning),
`docs/redesign-handoff.md` (the redesign's entry point).

## The problem this closes

The covers on LinkedIn and X were drawn **before** the pixel work. They wear
a smooth rounded-rect quilt with pill merges, in colours that no part of the
shipped product spends. Since then the app has grown a real colour system
(seven per-set palettes, three roles each) and a real pattern engine
(`lib/patterns.ts`). The channels are the last surface still speaking the
old language.

Three things already decided elsewhere are honoured here rather than
re-opened:

1. **No pill merges.** Malik cut them in the pattern lab's round 2 — they
   were "the weird part of the cover". Every quilt variant is one shape per
   cell.
2. **Straight edges stay ruler-straight; only curves rasterise**
   (`pixel-ui.md`, "the one rule").
3. **70/30 rhythm, accents never orthogonally adjacent** — the quilt
   family's shared rules, which the cover itself is the ancestor of.

## Division of labour — DECIDED (Malik, 2026-08-12)

> **The avatar carries the logo and nothing else. The cover carries the
> pattern and nothing else. Neither ever does both.**

This is what makes the pair compose. At the size a feed renders them, a
patterned avatar and a cover with a logo on it compete for the same job and
the reader gets two half-legible marks instead of one mark and one field.

Two consequences worth naming:

- **The cover needs no safe area.** Both platforms punch the avatar through
  the cover's bottom-left and X re-crops the header at narrow widths. A
  cover that is pure field survives every one of those crops; a cover with a
  lockup on it has to be designed around them.
- **The name is already on the page.** The account name, handle and bio all
  render directly under the cover. A wordmark in the artwork is the third
  time the reader is told what the product is called.

**The lab no longer has `Lockup` or `Composition` dials, and their absence
is a decision rather than an omission** (Malik, 2026-08-12). They existed so
a cover could be judged with type on it: Panel / Plate / Band clearings and
a mark-plus-wordmark-plus-claim lockup. Once the covers were settled as
pattern-only — and once the Open Graph card, the only canvas that genuinely
wanted type, was cut — every composition resolved to full bleed and the
lockup never rendered. Two dead dials are worse than none: they advertise
choices that do not exist.

What that leaves is a model with exactly two shapes. **A cover is a ground
plus a pattern. The avatar is a ground plus the mark.** The type machinery
is in git if it is ever wanted again.

Judge the pair, never the parts: the lab's **Composed · X profile** and
**Composed · LinkedIn page** put the cover and the avatar together.

**Those mocks render at the platform's own CSS pixel size, 1:1** — X's
600px column with a 133.33px avatar, LinkedIn's 1128px cover with a 200px
logo — rather than at a convenient preview width. The first version
estimated the ratios off a screenshot and put X's avatar at 14% of the
header's width. It is **22%**. Missing a third of the object is exactly the
error that lets a cover pass in the lab and crowd on the page, so the
geometry now comes from the platforms' published layouts, lives in one
`PLATFORMS` object, and the guide overlay on the standalone canvases is
derived from the same numbers.

## Pattern — Quilt · pixel

`quiltPixBody` from `lib/patterns.ts`, the dormant-but-ported member of the
quilt family. It is the direct pixel translation of the existing cover: same
cell walk, same 70/30 rhythm, same no-orthogonal-neighbours rule, with every
shape rebuilt from whole 7×7 sprite pixels.

Note what this makes true — **the covers are generated by the code the quiz
screens run.** `scripts/brand-assets.mjs` imports `lib/patterns.ts`
directly (Node strips the types), so there is no separate marketing
renderer to drift. `docs/brand-lab.html` carries a hand-port of the same
generator only so it can run over `file://` with no build step; if the lab
and the script ever disagree, **the lab is what's wrong.**

One cover-only deviation, applied as a transform so the generator stays
byte-identical: **the lattice is centred.** The generators lay cells from
(0,0) and run one column and row past the canvas, so a card ends with
whatever sliver falls at its right edge. On a quiz card nobody notices; on a
1500px cover a 3px orphan column reads as a printing error where a half-cell
reads as a crop.

### The dial, after cuts

All cut by Malik, 2026-08-12, and none worth carrying as a comparison:
**Quilt · smooth** (the outgoing cover's construction), **Camo · classic**,
and a **can pattern** built from `app/icon.svg` — one can per cell, a
quarter of them turned 90°. The can idea is not dead, but that execution
did not work and is gone from the lab.

What remains: **Quilt · pixel** (the recommendation, and what `docs/brand/`
ships) and **Camo · giant** (what the hard sets wear on their start
screens).

**Scale is 1.53** (Malik, 2026-08-12), against 1.00 on the first pass: the
quilt at cover size read as a mosaic rather than a pattern. LinkedIn's
191px strip takes 0.6 of that, or its cells are so tall the cover becomes
two rows of half-cropped shapes.

## Colour — the shipped accents

**The objective was wrong on the first pass, and the correction matters
more than the answer.**

That pass reasoned toward the chord the _existing_ cover wears — coral,
magenta, indigo, gold, purple — and called the match "continuity of chord".
Malik: those are the pre-redesign colours, and replacing them is the whole
point. So the objective was inverted; the work was optimising for the thing
being retired. Worth naming as a general trap: **when a redesign replaces a
palette, the old artefact is evidence of what the constraints were, never a
target to hit.**

What survives the correction is the measurement, which never depended on
the old cover. On a cream ground with the brand green dominant, **six of
the seven set surfaces fail** the quilt's own accent gate: they sit
0.045–0.157 from the cream in OKLab against a 0.17 floor, so they read as a
dirty ground rather than as confetti. Only J's lilac clears, at 0.176.

| candidate | hex       | ΔE vs cream | verdict |
| --------- | --------- | ----------- | ------- |
| L surface | `#CFF6DD` | 0.045       | fails   |
| C surface | `#E7F099` | 0.098       | fails   |
| Q surface | `#D9CCF9` | 0.106       | fails   |
| N surface | `#9EDAFF` | 0.122       | fails   |
| R surface | `#E4BDF7` | 0.137       | fails   |
| A surface | `#FFABC6` | 0.157       | fails   |
| J surface | `#E6ACF4` | 0.176       | clears  |

So the inversion is **forced, not chosen**: the quiz screens spend surfaces;
the brand spends the deep register. The pale tier cannot carry a
cream-ground cover whatever chord you happen to want.

**RECOMMENDED: the five shipped accents.** Every one is a colour the app
paints today, and all five clear the gate on cream comfortably:

| role    | hex       | set  | ΔE vs cream | ΔE vs green |
| ------- | --------- | ---- | ----------- | ----------- |
| ochre   | `#674900` | A, J | 0.522       | 0.253       |
| magenta | `#BD00AD` | C, L | 0.469       | 0.423       |
| purple  | `#8D0381` | N    | 0.540       | 0.409       |
| gold    | `#745400` | Q    | 0.483       | 0.223       |
| rust    | `#824616` | R    | 0.493       | 0.263       |

They sit at OKLCH L .38–.48 — a coherent mid-value tier, deep enough to
hold against the green without the holes that the inks punch. The result is
**deliberately quieter and more earthy than the outgoing cover**: ochre,
rust and gold where it had coral and bright violet. That is the palette the
product actually has.

Also in the lab, for judging: **Shipped inks** (the six, L .20–.35 — more
editorial, heavier), **Accents + inks** (the whole tier), and
**Everything** (all catalogue colours that clear the gate, including
retired ones — useful as a reference, not as a proposal).

Ground `#EDEDE3` and dominant `#05A24B` are `Crm` from the colour catalogue,
the entry already flagged `brand: true` and described there as "the social
cover's cream".

## Showing the sets' own colours — an inversion, not a tweak

Malik, 2026-08-12: the brand schemes should carry more of the sets'
colours. The obstacle is the one this lab opened with, and it does not
yield to turning a dial.

**A student knows a set by its SURFACE** — the surface is the whole screen
while they drill it. Set C is chartreuse; Set N is sky. But the surfaces
are pale, so against a pale ground they fail the quilt's own gate. Measured,
out of seven:

| ground                | set surfaces that read |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| `#EDEDE3` cream       | **1** of 7             |
| `#CFF6DD` Set L mint  | **3** of 7             |
| `#05A24B` brand green | **7** of 7             |

So the fix is to **put the brand green underneath them**. The green sits at
OKLCH L .61 and every surface at .87–.94, which is exactly the separation
the gate is asking for. That is `Brand · field`: green ground, deep teal as
the dominant shape (4.29:1), and all seven sets as confetti in the colours
they are actually recognised by.

It also changes what the cover _says_. On cream, the brand colour is one
mark among many; on `Brand · field` it is the ground the whole palette is
laid on — which is a more accurate description of the product than the
cream version was.

**Cost, and it is real:** the avatar is the same green, so on the composed
X profile the mark loses separation from the cover and leans on its white
ring to hold the edge. Letting the avatar keep a cream ground while the
cover goes green would fix it and break the one-scheme model. Not decided.

## Provenance: Set L, and why it is not a rival to the green

Malik picked `Set L` because its colour is close to the original LogiCola's.
That checks out, and the chain is stronger than "close":

- **Set L's mint `#CFF6DD` → Gensler's own help-page ground `#BBFFFF`:
  ΔE 0.045.** That is _below_ this project's own 0.17 "too close to tell
  apart" floor, and it is the closest of all seven surfaces — the next
  (Set N) is 2.4× further away.
- **The brand green `#05A24B` → VGA Green `#008000`: ΔE 0.106.** The
  original's Windows 3.1 build shipped a scheme literally named "Forest
  green".
- **Set L's mint → the brand green: 7° apart in hue** (157° vs 150°).

So mint and the brand green are one hue family at two lightnesses, and both
have ancestors in the original. Set L is not an alternative to the brand
green — it is the pale end of it. `Brand · mint` in the lab is that
reading: Gensler's ground, the brand green on top, a near-monochrome and
the most conservative option here.

## The wordmark now exists as a vector

`public/logicola-wordmark.svg` — added here, because the repo did not have
one. What was on disk before: `public/lc_logo.gif` (32px raster),
`public/icon-512.png` (raster, and a **different** mark — a can silhouette
on a mint plate), and `app/icon.svg` (the favicon, a third object). The
wordmark itself lived only in Figma.

That file is canonical. `scripts/brand-assets.mjs` reads it; the lab holds
an inline copy only so it keeps working over `file://`, where a fetch or a
module import would be blocked. **Edit the SVG, then mirror it into the
lab — never the other way round.**

**The lettering is a hole, not white ink.** The mark is recoloured through
two roles — body and knockout — and the knockout is always the scheme's
ground. A white option was built and cut (Malik, 2026-08-12): on cream it
is a barely-there off-white that reads as a printing fault rather than a
decision, and on any set surface it puts a colour in the mark that the
scheme does not own.

## It is a can, not a cartridge

Malik, 2026-08-12. Worth its own heading because **the misnomer is already
in the codebase**: `app/globals.css` describes the `corner-notch` utility's
shape as "matched to the Logicola cartridge's own corner". `docs/pixel-ui.md`
inherits the same word.

Left unfixed — renaming a comment in shipped CSS is not this change's job —
but flagged, because a wrong name for the central object of a brand
propagates into design decisions made months later by someone reading the
comment rather than looking at the mark.

## Pinning, and why the mocks are furniture-complete

Two working notes on the lab itself.

**Two dials that lied, fixed 2026-08-12** — found after Malik had pinned
twelve looks at `Accent 55%`, a setting that did nothing to nine of them.
`camoBody` never reads `rate` (its mix comes from the noise thresholds), so
the slider is now disabled under Camo and says why. Under the quilt it
worked but overstated itself: the no-orthogonal-adjacency rule rejects a
share of the cells that pass the roll, so a requested 55% lands at ~26%.
The note now reports the measured figure. Camo also spends only `pool[0]`
regardless of pool size, so the pool note names the one colour it will use.

And the slider's max of 55 was arbitrary — it, not the adjacency rule, was
the binding constraint on "can this be more colourful?". The rule alone
tops out near **52%** measured. Max is now 100, with a note when a setting
leaves the quilt family's documented 70/30 rhythm.

**Pins.** Six dials produce more combinations than the eye can hold, and
flipping between two of them from memory means choosing the one you saw
last rather than the one you liked. `Pin current` snapshots the six values
and renders them as the composed X cover — header plus avatar in place — so
saved looks are compared the way the pair is actually seen. They persist in
`localStorage`, with a silent fallback to memory because a page opened over
`file://` can be refused storage outright.

**The LinkedIn mock is now its own layout.** It and the X mock shared one
template with an `isX` ternary, and LinkedIn quietly inherited X's
furniture: action buttons pinned top-right, no tab row, no follows line.
LinkedIn puts its buttons _below_ the text, flush with the logo's left
edge, and closes the card with Home / About / Posts / Jobs / People. The
furniture is not decoration — it is what the cover is judged against, and a
cover that reads well under the wrong page reads differently under the
right one. Sharing a template across two page designs makes the second one
wrong without anyone noticing.

## What is not here

**No Open Graph / link card** (cut by Malik, 2026-08-12). It was in the
first pass because 1200x630 is the third social image a project usually
owns, but it is not a social _profile_ asset — it is the product site's
share image, it answers to different constraints, and it is the one surface
that genuinely wants a headline on it. Bundling it in was what kept the
type machinery alive in a lab about pattern and colour.
`public/thumbnail.jpg` is still the live link card and is untouched.

**Brand · mint is dead as a scheme** (Malik, 2026-08-14, nav lab
session): "we can kill this one here and everywhere else. I don't like
it." Said on seeing green-on-mint carry the exercises nav in
`docs/nav-lab.html` § 4. Removed there; retired from the marketing
lab's scheme dial the same day (kept as a comment beside `brandField`,
the same burial). The provenance argument (mint ≈ Gensler's help-page
ground, 7° from the brand green) was real — provenance is not a
verdict. Scope: this kills the mint-ground-with-green-ink _scheme_;
Set L's palette (plum ink on the same mint) remains a live primary
candidate, and the mint plates baked into `app/icon.svg` /
`public/icon-512.png` are separate objects untouched by this call.

## Regenerating

```bash
pnpm brand:assets          # the decided set, to docs/brand/
pnpm brand:assets --all    # every scheme, for judging
```

Three files: `logicola-x-header.svg` (1500x500),
`logicola-linkedin-cover.svg` (1128x191), `logicola-avatar.svg` (400x400).

SVG is the master. PNGs need `npx playwright install chromium` once — or use
the PNG buttons in the lab, which need nothing installed.
