What makes a well-formed formula (wff)?

Every formula is built by the three formation rules. Anything the rules can't build isn't a wff.

B Any capital letter is a wff on its own.
∼A The result of prefixing any wff with "∼" is a wff.
(A · B) The result of joining two wffs with "·", "∨", "⊃" or "≡" — wrapped in parentheses — is a wff.

Use a pair of parentheses for each:

(A · B)and — both are true.
(A ∨ B)or — at least one is true.
(A ⊃ B)if-then — if A, then B.
(A ≡ B)if and only if.

Reading practice

∼(P · Q)Not both P and Q.
(∼P · Q)P is false and Q is true.
∼(P ∨ Q)Neither P nor Q.
(∼P ∨ Q)Either P is false or Q is true.
(P ⊃ ∼Q)If P, then Q is false.
∼(P ⊃ Q)It's false that if P then Q.
((P · Q) ⊃ R)If both P and Q, then R.
(P ⊃ (Q ∨ R))If P, then Q or R.
∼∼PNot not-P — which is just P, with attitude.

Bottom-sheet redesign — what this lab tests

2026-08-21 · after user testing. References: Apple Stocks (detented sheet, symbol card with ✕), Spotify Now Playing (fixed page + scrollable content, not a sheet).

Decided — 2026-08-21

  • Flick zone is the gesture. Dial retired; the bottom-30% flick is wired in every sheet anatomy.
  • Dragger: iOS narrow (36×5) is the default. Chevron dropped — it read as a button, not a handle.
  • Scroll-lock CTA lives in the exercise flow — Spotify's play button, above the guide, never sticky.
  • THE DECISION (user-tested): classic anatomy — grabber above the CTA, iOS-narrow dragger, CTA hides when open as a 200ms opacity + blur morph. Split tab row retired (didn't work); pixel caps retired. Corner finding for the record: it improves when the CTA footer leaves with the tab — implemented and archived on the variant.
  • Bug Malik caught as "a divider that doesn't need to be there": the empty footer painted its top rule in every anatomy — the hidden attribute is UA-level display:none, and an author display:flex silently defeats it. Worth checking for in the product port wherever [hidden] meets a display utility.

H1 — the CTA should not ride up with the sheet

The collapsed sheet and the expanded sheet are different surfaces with different jobs: footer (act) vs reader (look things up). Today one header serves both, so Check Answer becomes a top-bar button floating above the guide — the confusion user testing caught. Hypothesis: swapping it for a title row when expanded removes the confusion at the cost of one extra gesture (collapse first, then check). Since the options are covered by the open sheet anyway, that gesture was already required in practice.

  • Measure: taps on Check while the sheet is expanded (should go to ~0), and time from guide-open back to answer.
  • Middle option — "hides at full only" — keeps the CTA at the 460px snap where part of the card is still visible. Feel whether that's useful or just the old confusion at one snap fewer.

H2 — gesture surface: halo vs flick zone

A swipe up and a scroll down are the same motion, and the quiz card scrolls. Two candidates:

  • Halo — extend the drag surface an invisible 40px above the sheet edge. Bigger target, zero conflicts. The conservative bet.
  • Flick zone — a fast upward flick (velocity-gated) in the bottom 30% opens the sheet; slow drags pass through to scroll. Feels native when it works, but on the web the browser competes for the gesture: once native scroll claims the touch the page may stop hearing it, and both can fire on the same swipe. This lab implements it honestly, so the misfires you feel are the misfires users would feel.
  • Platform reality: the bottom edge itself belongs to iOS — home indicator swipes and Safari's toolbar live there. "Swipe up from the bottom of the screen" can never be literal in a web app.

H3 — the ✕ chip

Found while reading the shipped code: the grabber tap cycles forward only (collapsed → guide → full → collapsed). From the guide snap, tapping takes you fuller; the only way down is a drag. The ✕ is a direct, discoverable "down" (Stocks symbol-card precedent) — and it strengthens the single-pointer alternative to dragging that WCAG 2.5.7 asks for. Note the cycle itself is HIG-blessed ("they can also tap it to cycle through the detents"); the hole is the missing explicit way down before the cycle wraps.

H4 — anatomy: where do the CTA and the affordance live?

Five architectures on dial 1. Grabber below CTA keeps today's structure but flips the reading order — footer first, pull-tab under it. The catch: both platforms put the drag affordance at the sheet's top edge, and the bottom strip belongs to the system (home indicator on iOS, gesture nav on Android), so the tab lives in contested territory. Feel whether grabbing it is even reliable. The split family takes Check Answer out of the sheet entirely: a fixed footer that never moves, plus a reference surface expanding above it. This is the cleanest reading of "different surfaces, different jobs" — the CTA stays reachable while the guide is open, without ever riding the sheet — and it matches how the platforms think: Material's standard sheet holds supplementary content; the screen's primary action stays with the screen.

H5 — the banding problem (why the split has three variants)

The first split draft stacked five horizontal bands when collapsed: sheet rule, grabber, label, footer rule, CTA. Each variant deletes a band and asks what it cost:

  • Tab row — grabber and label share one 34px line. Two rules on screen. Cheapest fix; still a full-width strip.
  • Corner tab — the resting sheet surface disappears; a right-docked pixel tab (file-folder grammar) rides the footer. One full-width rule. The question: does a corner affordance read as "pull me", and is it reachable left-handed?
  • Footer button — no resting sheet at all; the guide is a book chip beside the CTA, the same glyph as the top chrome's Guide chip (one door, two locations). One rule total — the quietest screen. The cost: the drag affordance is gone at rest (tap or flick summons the sheet), and the guide loses its always-visible presence. M3 would call this a button-triggered modal sheet rather than a standard sheet — a different pattern, not a variant of the same one.
  • Refinements 2026-08-21: the split tab collapses to a label-only row (pill + text inline read as jarring; the pill returns stacked above the label when expanded); the corner tab wears the book icon and crossfades with the sheet surface instead of popping; grabber-below hides its top rule at rest, surfacing it only as the sheet expands.

Dial 2 explores the dragger itself: the shipped 100×8 pill is nearly 3× the iOS system grabber (36×5). "Pixel caps" borrows the desktop grip's stair-capped grammar. The chevron is the only one that signifies direction — it points where tapping goes and flips when open — at the price of looking like a button rather than a handle.

Platform guidelines — checked 2026-08-21

Apple HIG, "Sheets":

  • The grabber is "a small horizontal indicator that can appear at the top edge of a sheet". Resizable sheets should include one; tapping it cycles through detents, and it works with VoiceOver.
  • A sheet "expands when people scroll its contents or drag the grabber" — the two sanctioned expand gestures. Nothing about gestures on the parent view.
  • "Support swiping to dismiss" AND provide a Cancel/Close button — both, not either. Placement: "the Cancel button belongs on the leading edge" (Stocks' ✕ is top-left; this lab's chip is top-right, matching the desktop pane — placement is an open call).
  • Nonmodal sheets are a first-class iOS pattern (Notes formatting) — the persistent quiz sheet is one.

Material 3, "Bottom sheets":

  • Our sheet maps to a standard (non-modal) bottom sheet: it "co-exists with the screen's main UI region" for "simultaneously viewing and interacting with both".
  • "At full-screen height, standard bottom sheets contain a collapse icon in an app bar to return to their initial position." — M3 flat-out requires the H3 affordance at full.
  • "Selecting the drag handle should toggle through preset heights or close the sheet"; and without a drag handle, "Material requires the inclusion of a single-pointer alternative to change height."
  • Dismissal/expansion gestures all act on the sheet itself (drag handle, sheet surface, its scrolling content, the scrim). Neither platform defines an off-sheet gesture zone — the flick zone is off-guideline on both. Not forbidden, just ours to justify with testing.

What Spotify actually teaches

Not the sheet — the full state. At full expansion the surface should behave like a page: fixed header, scrolling content, and drag-down moves the sheet only when the content is scrolled to its top. The sim implements exactly that (so does Stocks; so does vaul).

H6 — scroll lock, and what the honest mock changed

The scroll-lock anatomy is the Spotify reading taken literally: no sheet, no drag machinery — the guide is a section below the exercise in one scroller, CSS scroll-snap locks the viewport to either state, the guide's heading peeks at rest, and the CTA is Spotify's play button: part of the exercise screen, above the guide, scrolling away when you commit to reading (so Check Answer is off-screen while the guide is open — scroll back to act). Because it is not a sheet, none of the sheet grammar survives: no grabber, no ✕ — the guide's label is a section heading, and the way back is the scroll itself (dials 2–5 grey out accordingly). Its virtues: zero gesture invention (scrolling is the one gesture every user already has), no misfire budget, and the snap gives the "state" feel without a single pointer handler. Its costs: the guide can arrive by accident on an overscroll; there is no explicit dismiss for users who want one; and mid-states don't exist — there is no half-open guide.

Rebuilding the mock to the real exercise window also changed a verdict: the REAL list-set screen centres one question and four options in the viewport — it does not scroll. The flick zone's scroll conflict therefore mostly evaporates on list sets; where it survives is grid sets (Set R), whose options region scrolls internally in exactly the zone the flick would claim. And the real chrome already carries a 44×44 Guide chip in the top row — any new affordance is a second door to the same room, so the question is which door is primary, not whether one exists.
